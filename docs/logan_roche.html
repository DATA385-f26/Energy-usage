Data Collection Facts Label

Data Source Facts Label
  1. Source Identity
Source name 2020 Residential Energy Consumption Survey (RECS) Public Use Microdata File, version 7 (Jan 2024), U.S. Energy Information Administration. https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/index.php?view=microdata
Domain/topic Energy (residential energy consumption and housing)
Source type Government dataset (federal statistical survey, public-use microdata)
  1. Collection Context
Collector/producer U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), the statistical and analytical agency within the U.S. Department of Energy (public, federal). The Household Survey was conducted with private contractors IMG-Crown and RTI International; the Energy Supplier Survey with private contractors Leidos and Westat.
Original purpose Periodic national survey to provide detailed information about energy usage in U.S. homes: a reference-year snapshot of energy characteristics, usage patterns, and household demographics. Used for public reporting (data tables, reports) and as input to EIA’s sector-level projections such as the Annual Energy Outlook.
Collection method Combination of methods: (1) voluntary, self-administered Household Survey (form EIA-457A) by web or paper, in English or Spanish, mailed to an address-based sample; (2) mandatory Energy Supplier Survey (EIA-457D-G) where utilities and fuel suppliers submitted billing/delivery records via a web form or Excel file; (3) engineering-based end-use models calibrated to billing totals; (4) weather data from NOAA Climate Data Online linked to each home. Missing survey answers filled by hot-deck imputation.
Record-creation event A sampled household completes the Household Survey (at least 7 of 10 key questions answered). The record is then linked to that household’s supplier billing data, modeled end-use estimates, and weather data.
  1. Observation and Scope
Unit of observation One occupied housing unit used as a primary residence (one responding household), identified by DOEID.
Population or objects represented All occupied housing units used as primary residences in the 50 states and DC (about 123.5 million). Excludes vacant homes, seasonal/second homes, and group quarters (dorms, nursing homes, prisons, barracks); housing units on military installations are included. Sample: 18,496 responding households.
Time period covered Consumption and expenditures cover calendar year 2020 (Jan 1 - Dec 31, 2020). Household Survey fielded Sept-Nov 2020 and Jan-Apr 2021. Supplier data collected July 2021 - March 2022, covering bills from March 2019 - Feb 2021. Final file released January 2024.
Geographic area coverage United States: all 50 states and the District of Columbia. Public file includes census region, census division, state, climate region/zone, and urban/rural type; addresses and localized geography are removed.
  1. Variables and Data Structure
Main variables/fields Housing characteristics (housing type, year built, square footage); appliances and electronics; space heating, air conditioning, and water heating equipment and fuels; thermostat settings; lighting; household characteristics (number of members, income, own/rent); energy assistance; electric vehicles and solar; telework/online school (COVID); heating and cooling degree days; annual consumption and cost by fuel (electricity, natural gas, propane, fuel oil); end-use consumption estimates (e.g., space heating, cooling); sampling weight NWEIGHT and 60 replicate weights.
Data types included Numerical (consumption, cost, square footage, degree days, weights, counts); categorical (coded survey responses, income ranges, fuel and equipment types); IDs (DOEID); geographic categories (state, region, climate zone); imputation flag variables. No GPS, image, or audio data.
Direct measurements Respondent-reported answers (e.g., number of household members, housing type, thermostat setpoints, laundry loads per week); supplier-reported billed energy consumption and dollars spent (for households with billing data); weather-station temperatures used to compute degree days.
Possible proxy variables Modeled end-use consumption estimates; heating and cooling degree days; climate region/zone; year-built range; household income category; urban/rural type; energy insecurity / energy assistance questions.
  1. Documentation
Documentation found Variable and response codebook (XLSX); ‘Using the microdata file to compute estimates and standard errors’ guide; Household Characteristics Technical Documentation Summary; Consumption and Expenditures Technical Documentation Summary; Square Footage Methodology; ‘Comparing the 2020 RECS with previous RECS’ report; survey forms EIA-457 A-G; RECS FAQs and terminology pages.
Stated collection details Address-based sample from the USPS delivery file, stratified by state (about 99.6% expected frame coverage). Up to six mailings over about six weeks; $10 incentive for web response. 18,496 completes: 72.8% web, 27.2% paper; median web completion time 32 minutes. Response rate 38.6% unweighted / 37.9% weighted (state range 30.4% NY to 50.7% WI). About 240 public-file variables had imputed values (median imputation rate 2.7%, range 0-22.1%). Supplier survey: 94% of suppliers responded; 84% overall household account coverage (about 50% for propane and fuel oil). For electricity, 83% of households had complete billing data and 14% had none; households with no billing data had consumption imputed from models. Survey answers were edited to reconcile with billing data (e.g., main heating fuel). Weights adjusted for eligibility, nonresponse, and poststratification to 2020 Census and 2019 ACS totals; 60 jackknife replicate weights. Wood use is self-reported only and not included in totals. Confidentiality protected under CIPSEA; addresses removed and some variables top-coded.
